Salem, Oregon

Agility is the anchor. The Convention Center is the arena. This is the hometown games.

We are not importing a robotics weekend into a random hall. The T1000 Invitational sits in Salem because Agility sits in Salem. Digit is assembled at RoboFab on Truax Drive — the first full-scale humanoid factory in the world — about ten minutes from the Salem Convention Center at 200 Commercial Street SE.

China just ran 2,056 robots through a Beijing oval. That was industrial policy with an audience. Our version is a public Olympics in the city that already builds the machines — and we invite the teams that just ran Beijing to come score on an American floor.

Four classes: invited Chinese teams, high school, college, and open. Same arenas. Separate podiums. Spectators welcome.

The field

Four classes. One Robot Olympics.

China’s games were almost a closed medal table — 641 of 666 teams were domestic. Salem invites that field here, then puts it next to Oregon high schools, college labs, and anyone who can build a machine that makes the rulebook. Events are shared. Scoring and podiums are by class.

Class I · Invite

China / International

Formal invitations to teams off the World Humanoid Robot Games and World Robot Conference lists — Unitree, X-Humanoid / TianGong, AgiBot, university alliances, and other platforms that want a public U.S. score. Same course. No exhibition pass. They come to compete.

Class II · Schools

High School

Oregon FIRST, VEX, and independent high-school teams, plus visiting prep programs. Kit bots and custom frames. The pipeline class — pits next to Digit so a sophomore from North Salem can see the factory job, not just a trophy.

Class III · Labs

College

OSU Dynamic Robotics Lab as the spiritual home. Open to UO, PSU, OIT, UW, Stanford, CMU, and any accredited lab that can trailer a platform. Research stacks welcome. Still has to finish the course.

Class IV · Anyone

Open Class

Independent builders, garage teams, industry prototypes that are not the invited Chinese field, veterans of combat circuits. No age cap. If it inspects and it moves, it runs.

Compete in multiple events

Six arenas. One humanoid floor.

Every class can enter the six T1000 arenas. The Agility floor is the humanoid and work-task course — the place invited Chinese platforms and Digit share a public clock.

01

Combat Arena

Weaponized bots in a walled cage. Sparks, wedges, drums. Weight classes posted with the rulebook. Last bot moving takes the heat.

02

Sumo Showdown

Push-out on a raised dohyo. Autonomous or driven. The cleanest event on the card and the loudest when a favorite goes over the edge.

03

Line Following

Sensor craft. Tight course, timed runs, penalties for leaves. The classroom-to-podium event — open to every age.

04

Obstacle Course

Ramps, gaps, stairs, gates. Built so a 3-pound kit and a university platform can both look heroic.

05

Speed Challenge

Straight-line and slalom clocks. The number the cameras want. Humanoid sprint exhibition runs the same weekend.

06

Freestyle Performance

Choreography, design, crowd work. Dance, demo, story. Judged on control, originality, and whether the room stands up.

Industry anchor · Agility Robotics

The games are built around the company that already lives here.

Agility is the anchor tenant of this event the way RoboFab is the anchor factory of this industry in Oregon. Spun out of OSU’s Dynamic Robotics Lab in 2015. Headquartered in Salem. Digit 5 unveiled September 15, 2026 — 50-pound lift, 7.2-foot reach, 20-hour workday, built to share a floor with people. More than 65,000 hours already worked at GXO, Schaeffler, Amazon, and Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada. Peak RoboFab capacity: 10,000 humanoids a year.

Anchor role in year one: name on the humanoid class, Digit on the exhibition floor, safety brief co-authored with their engineers, high-school and college pits that walk students toward that shop instead of toward Fremont, and a standing invite to the Chinese field that just filled Beijing.

Agility · Digit · RoboFab

4698 Truax Drive SE, Salem. Ten minutes from the Convention Center. The humanoid class is not a guest demo dropped onto a combat weekend. It is the Salem reason the T1000 exists as a Robot Olympics instead of another school tournament.

What China just did

August 2026, Beijing. 666 teams. 2,056 robots. 51 events. A 100-meter in 8.64 seconds. Almost every medal stayed home. It was industrial policy with a sold-out oval.

What Salem can do

A two-day public Olympics downtown, anchored by Agility, with RoboFab ten minutes south. Combat, classrooms, and a humanoid that already works for a living. Keep the talent — and the next software building — in Salem.

Downtown Salem Convention Center as the public arena. RoboFab on Truax as the industrial backstage. Agility as the named anchor. Mid-June so schools are out. Dates move if the venue or Agility lock a different weekend. The geography does not.
